## Reference basis

Normal anatomy and identifying markings follow [Michigan DNR's fish identification guide](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/-/media/Project/Websites/dnr/Documents/LED/digests/2024_fishing_guide.pdf?rev=5848d29f4ba544d8bd028998a7abfeeb), its species pages for [bluegill](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/bluegill), [crappie](https://www.michigan.gov/en/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/crappie), [walleye](https://www.michigan.gov/dnr/education/michigan-species/fish-species/walleye), and [USFWS yellow perch photography](https://www.fws.gov/media/yellow-perch-2). Black crappie spots and fin shape also follow [Missouri Department of Conservation](https://mdc.mo.gov/discover-nature/field-guide/black-crappie).

Reported albino color photographs were located for [northern pike (Robin Bue, July 2026)](https://www.timminstoday.com/local-news/rare-albino-northern-pike-caught-three-times-12577778) and [muskellunge (American Legacy Fishing, Bob Bowman photo)](https://www.flickr.com/photos/gloomisus/6684729293/). These are visual palette references, not genetic confirmation. Bluegill, yellow perch, black crappie, largemouth bass, walleye and lake sturgeon variants use attempted melanin-loss palettes (ivory/cream, faint pink fins and red/pink eyes), because a reliable confirmed species-specific albino color reference was not found. Leucistic, xanthic and ordinary pale fish were not treated as confirmed albinos. Anatomy remains species-specific in every variant.
